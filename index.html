Understanding Tribunal Deadlines and Compensation

How the 3-Month Limitation Period Works

In most UK employment tribunal claims — including unfair dismissal, constructive dismissal, and discrimination — you have three months less one day from the date of dismissal (or the date of the act you are complaining about) to submit your claim. For example, if you were dismissed on 15 January, your deadline would ordinarily fall on 14 April. This tight timeframe catches many people off guard, which is why checking your deadline early is essential.

How ACAS Early Conciliation Affects Your Deadline

Before filing most tribunal claims, you must contact ACAS (the Advisory, Conciliation and Arbitration Service) for early conciliation. This process effectively pauses the limitation clock. The period between the date ACAS receives your conciliation notification and the date they issue your certificate is added back onto your deadline. In practice, this means your final deadline may be several weeks later than the standard three-month date. Always keep your ACAS certificate safe — it contains the key dates you will need.

Employment Rights Act 2025 — Current Reforms

Important: The Employment Rights Act 2025 received Royal Assent in December 2025 and is now law. It is being implemented in phases through 2026 and 2027. Key changes include: the statutory cap on the unfair dismissal compensatory award is due to be abolished from 1 January 2027, and a planned extension of tribunal time limits (expected to move from 3 months to 6 months) is part of the reforms but has not yet come into force. Until then, the current 3-month-less-one-day rule (with ACAS adjustments) still applies. Always check the latest position before relying on any deadline or compensation estimate.

Basic Award vs Compensatory Award vs Vento Bands

Unfair dismissal compensation is made up of several components:

  • Basic Award — A formula-based statutory payment calculated from your age, length of service, and gross weekly pay (capped). It mirrors the statutory redundancy calculation: 0.5 week's pay per year under age 22, 1 week per year aged 22–40, and 1.5 weeks per year aged 41+.
  • Compensatory Award — Designed to cover your actual financial loss (lost earnings, benefits, pension contributions). This is subject to a statutory cap: currently the lower of £123,543 or 52 weeks' gross pay (for dismissals on or after 6 April 2026). The estimate depends heavily on individual circumstances, including whether you have found new employment.
  • Vento Bands — These apply specifically to injury to feelings in discrimination cases. They are split into three tiers: lower band (one-off or minor incidents), middle band (serious or continuing conduct), and upper band (ongoing significant impact). The bands are updated annually and are separate from — and in addition to — the basic and compensatory awards.

The total compensation in a discrimination case can therefore include all three elements, potentially resulting in a significantly higher overall award. Our calculator provides a broad estimate; for a detailed assessment tailored to your situation, speak with an employment law specialist.
